Planning poker for remote teams

Remote estimation fails when people talk over each other or wait for the loudest voice. A shared room with hidden cards keeps everyone's first opinion honest, wherever they sit.

What a remote session needs

  • One link, no accounts. People join from the meeting chat in seconds, on a laptop or a phone.
  • Hidden votes and one reveal. Nobody anchors on the first number said out loud.
  • A shared timer. The discussion after a split vote stays short.
  • Notes from the lowest and highest voters. Quiet people get heard without having to interrupt.

Across time zones

With async voting, everyone votes on the tickets before the meeting; the votes stay hidden until the reveal. The call then only covers the tickets the team disagrees on.

Inside the tools you already use

Start a room from Slack with /poker, or add it as a tab to a Microsoft Teams meeting. See the integrations.
