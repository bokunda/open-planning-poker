Planning poker cards and scales

The card deck is the scale your team estimates on. Each common deck has gaps that grow with the numbers, because bigger items are harder to estimate precisely. Here is how the usual decks compare and when to use each.

The common decks

DeckCardsBest for
Fibonacci0, ½, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13Most teams estimating user stories in story points.
Modified Fibonacci0, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13, 20, 40, 100Backlogs with large items or epics that need a rough size.
T-shirt sizesXS, S, M, L, XL, XXLEarly, rough sizing, roadmaps, and teams new to estimating.
Powers of 21, 2, 4, 8, 16, 32, 64Teams that like each step to mean "double the size".

Why the gaps grow

The difference between a 1 and a 2 is easy to judge. The difference between a 21 and a 22 is not: nobody can estimate a large item that precisely. Scales like Fibonacci leave out the numbers in between, so the team only has to answer "is it more like 8 or more like 13?" A big gap is also a hint: if the team keeps choosing the top cards, the item should be split.

Fibonacci

Each number is roughly the sum of the two before it. The ½ card is for tiny changes such as a text fix. It is the default deck in Open Planning Poker and a good choice if your team is unsure.

Modified Fibonacci

The same start, but it rounds the larger values to 20, 40 and 100. Those cards say "big, and we know it is not precise". They are useful for backlog refinement, where some items are still epics.

T-shirt sizes

Sizes instead of numbers keep the discussion about relative size and away from hours. They work well for early planning. If you later need numbers for velocity, agree on a mapping, for example S = 2, M = 5, L = 8.

Powers of 2

Every card is double the previous one. Some teams find this the easiest scale to reason about; the trade-off is fewer options in the middle.

The special cards

  • ? (question): "I don't have enough information to estimate." Several of these mean the item is not ready.
  • Break: "I need a pause." Long sessions produce worse estimates, so take it seriously.
  • 0: the work is already done or trivial.

Custom decks

Some teams use hours, days or their own scale. In Open Planning Poker you pick a deck when you create a game, or type your own values; the preview shows the cards before you start. See how to run a session for the rest of the setup.
