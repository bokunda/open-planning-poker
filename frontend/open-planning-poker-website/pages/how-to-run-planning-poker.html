How to run a planning poker session

This guide walks through a planning poker session from preparation to the last estimate. It works the same for in-person, remote and hybrid teams; the examples use Open Planning Poker, which is free and needs no sign-up.

Before the session

Keep the list short. Ten to fifteen well-prepared items fit into an hour. A session that tries to estimate fifty items ends with tired guesses.

Have a reference item. Pick one finished item the whole team remembers and agree on its size, for example "the login page was a 3". New items are compared with it.

Step by step

  1. Prepare the items

    Pick the user stories or tickets the team will estimate, in priority order. Each should have a short description and acceptance criteria, so the team estimates the work rather than guessing what it is.

  2. Choose a card scale

    Agree on a scale before the session: Fibonacci (0, ½, 1, 2, 3, 5, 8, 13) for most teams, modified Fibonacci for bigger items, or T-shirt sizes for rough sizing.

  3. Create a room and invite the team

    Create a game, pick the deck and share the link or QR code. Everyone joins with a name; no accounts are needed.

  4. Add the tickets

    Add the items one by one or import a list from a CSV or JSON export of your tracker.

  5. Vote in secret

    The host reads the item and answers questions. Everyone picks a card. Nobody sees the values until the reveal.

  6. Reveal and discuss

    The host reveals all cards at once. If the votes are far apart, ask the people with the lowest and highest cards to explain, then vote again.

  7. Record the estimate and move on

    When the votes are close, record the estimate in your tracker and go to the next item. Export the session as a PDF if you need a record.

Handling disagreement

A wide spread is the most useful moment of the session. Keep the discussion to the two outliers first: they usually know something the others don't, such as a hidden dependency or an existing component that makes the work smaller. Then vote again. If the team still disagrees after two rounds, the item is probably unclear: note the open question and come back to it later.

Keeping it short

  • Use a timer for discussions. In Open Planning Poker the host starts a timer that everyone sees, and the cards are revealed when it runs out.
  • Use the "?" card when someone does not have enough information. Several "?" cards mean the item is not ready.
  • Use the break card when people need a pause; it is better than estimating while tired.

Remote and hybrid tips

  • Share the room link in the meeting chat and put the QR code on the shared screen, so people in the room can join from their phones.
  • Ask everyone to keep their camera on during discussions; it helps the outliers explain.
  • Let the host share the screen with the table, so the whole team sees the reveal at the same moment.
