Self-hosted planning poker

Open Planning Poker is open source (GPL-3.0). You can use it for free on openplanningpoker.com, or run it on your own servers when your company needs estimates, ticket names and chat to stay inside its network.

When to self-host

  • Your security policy does not allow ticket names or discussions on third-party services.
  • You want to connect it to internal tools, single sign-on or your own monitoring.
  • You want to change the app: decks, branding or new features.

The stack

PartTechnology
Web appAngular with server-side rendering (Node.js)
API.NET 10 GraphQL services (Game Engine, User Management) behind a HotChocolate Fusion gateway
Real-time updatesGraphQL subscriptions over WebSockets
StoragePostgreSQL for games, tickets and votes; Redis for usernames, chat and the timer
ObservabilityOpenTelemetry, Prometheus, Loki and Grafana (optional)

What you need

  • A Linux server or VM with Docker. A small instance (2 vCPU, 4 GB RAM) is enough for many teams.
  • A reverse proxy with TLS, such as nginx with Let's Encrypt, for the app and the GraphQL gateway.
  • A secret of at least 32 characters for signing the users' tokens.

Getting started

  1. Clone the repository from GitHub.
  2. Start PostgreSQL and Redis with Docker Compose.
  3. Start the Game Engine, User Management and the Fusion gateway, then the web app.
  4. Point your reverse proxy at the web app and the gateway.

The full, current instructions are in the setup guide and the deployment guide.

What data it stores

  • Usernames: random by default, and deleted after 24 hours.
  • Games, tickets and votes: kept in PostgreSQL, so teams can see past estimates and export a PDF report.
  • Chat: the last 100 messages per game, deleted after 24 hours.
  • No email addresses or passwords: players join with a link.
